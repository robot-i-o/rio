# Contributing to RIO

Thank you for your interest in contributing to RIO (Robot I/O)! RIO is an open-source project stewarded by **Lavoro AI, Inc.** ("Lavoro"), originally developed at Carnegie Mellon University. We welcome contributions of all kinds: new hardware drivers, teleoperation interfaces, middleware backends, bug fixes, documentation, and examples.

## Contributor License Agreement (CLA)

Before we can accept your first contribution, we need you to sign a Contributor License Agreement. The CLA protects you, the project, and everyone who uses RIO by making the intellectual-property status of every contribution clear. Signing the CLA does **not** transfer your copyright — you keep full ownership of your work and can use it however you like. It simply grants Lavoro and RIO's users a license to distribute your contribution as part of the project.

### Which CLA do I sign?

- **Individual CLA** — sign this if you are contributing on your own behalf and your employer has no claim to the work (see [`RIO_Individual_CLA.pdf`](./cla/RIO_Individual_CLA.pdf)).
- **Corporate CLA** — have your employer sign this if you are contributing as part of your job, or if your employer otherwise has rights to your work (see [`RIO_Corporate_CLA.pdf`](./cla/RIO_Corporate_CLA.pdf)). The Corporate CLA includes a **Schedule A** listing the employees authorized to contribute; your company's Point of Contact can add or remove names at any time by emailing us.

If you're not sure whether your employer has rights to your contributions (this often depends on your employment agreement and local law), please check with your employer before signing the Individual CLA.

### How to sign

1. Download and complete the appropriate CLA form (both are fillable PDFs).
2. Sign it — an electronic signature or a typed name with date is acceptable.
3. Email the completed form to **cla@lavoro.bot**.
4. We will confirm receipt, record your GitHub username, and your pull requests will be cleared for merging.

You only need to sign once; the CLA covers all your current and future contributions to RIO. If we adopt an automated CLA check (e.g., CLA Assistant on GitHub), you'll also be able to sign electronically directly from your first pull request.

### What the CLA covers (plain-language summary)

This summary is for convenience only — the signed agreement is what governs.

- **Copyright license:** you grant Lavoro and RIO's users a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free license to use, modify, and redistribute your contribution.
- **Patent license:** you grant a license to any of your patent claims that your contribution necessarily infringes, with a standard defensive-termination clause.
- **Your promises:** the contribution is your original work (or clearly marked third-party material with its license identified), and you have the right to submit it.
- **Our promise:** Lavoro will distribute contributions only under the project's open-source license (or other OSI-approved terms) — your contribution won't be taken proprietary out from under you.
- **No obligations:** you are not required to provide support, and your contribution is provided "as is."

### Third-party code

If you want to include code you didn't write (e.g., a vendor SDK snippet or code from another open-source project), don't fold it silently into a contribution. Submit it separately, clearly marked as third-party material, with its complete source and license details, per Section 7 of the CLA. Maintainers will review whether its license is compatible with RIO's.

## Provenance of the RIO codebase

The original RIO codebase was developed at Carnegie Mellon University and released as open source; Lavoro AI, Inc. stewards its continued development under the project's open-source license. All contributions accepted after the initial release are covered by signed CLAs under the process described above.

## Questions?

Open an issue, or contact the maintainers at **cla@lavoro.bot**. If you have legal questions about signing, please consult your own counsel or your employer's legal department.
